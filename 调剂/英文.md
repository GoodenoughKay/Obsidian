### Q2：Tell me about your PINN research.

```text
In that project, I studied two deep learning methods 
for solving the Burgers equation — a nonlinear PDE 
that develops shock waves even from smooth initial conditions.

The standard PINN method embeds the PDE residual 
directly into the loss function. It requires computing 
partial derivatives of the network output using 
automatic differentiation. The problem is that 
shock waves are discontinuities — the partial 
derivatives do not exist there. As a result, 
the network smooths over the shock instead of 
capturing it sharply. This is called smooth failure.

The IFNN method takes a different approach. 
It uses the implicit form of the solution: 
u equals phi of x minus u times t, 
where phi is the initial condition function. 
This equation does not require any derivatives — 
it only compares function values. So shock 
discontinuities do not affect training at all.

In my experiment with a smooth sinusoidal 
initial condition, the IFNN successfully captured 
the shock near x equals 0.45, while the PINN 
smoothed it out. The L2 error of IFNN was 
about two orders of magnitude lower.
```

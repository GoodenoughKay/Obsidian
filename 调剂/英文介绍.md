# 英语自我介绍——上科大材料与化工专硕复试专用

> 📌 这不是通用模板的填空，而是根据位凯瑞的真实背景、专门为上科大材料与化工跨考场景定制的英文自我介绍体系。包含三个版本（1分钟/2分钟/追问应答），以及所有可能的衍生问题的参考答案。

## 📋 使用说明

- **版本A（1分钟）**：大多数学校要求，直接背诵，控制在 60–70 秒
- **版本B（2分钟）**：如果老师说"请详细介绍一下自己"，用这个版本
- **关键原则**：不要背得太流利，像在自然说话。每隔几句可以有 0.5 秒的停顿，显得真实
- **地雷区**：不要用 "Let me introduce myself"（老师听烂了），直接开口

## 🎤 版本A：标准 1 分钟版（必背）

```text
Good morning, dear professors. I'm Wei Kairui, a final-year student 
majoring in Big Data Management and Applications at Shanghai Institute 
of Technology.

My undergraduate training gave me a solid foundation in machine 
learning, data structures, and systems programming. I also hold a 
CET-6 certificate and have practical experience in both Python and C++.

During my undergraduate years, I completed two research projects. 
The first involved applying physics-informed neural networks — PINNs — 
to solve hyperbolic conservation law equations, which are mathematically 
related to diffusion and phase-field models commonly used in materials 
science. The second was my graduation project: designing a 
high-performance vector search engine in C++17, optimized for the 
Apple M3 chip.

I'm applying to SHANGHAITECH because I'm genuinely excited about 
the intersection of computational methods and materials science. 
I believe my background in numerical methods and systems optimization 
gives me a unique perspective that I can contribute to your research group.

Thank you.
```

**朗读时长参考**：约 55–65 秒（正常语速）

**关键句解析**：

- "physics-informed neural networks — PINNs —"：破折号后加解释，万一老师没听懂 PINN，这句话本身就给了解释空间
- "mathematically related to diffusion and phase-field models"：这是你的核心桥梁句，主动把计算机经历和材料科学连起来
- "unique perspective that I can contribute"：传递"我能给你们带来价值"而不是"我需要你们教我"

## 🎤 版本B：详细 2 分钟版（备用）

```text
Good morning, dear professors. I'm Wei Kairui, 22 years old, 
from Xuzhou, Jiangsu Province. I'm currently a final-year student 
at Shanghai Institute of Technology, majoring in Big Data Management 
and Applications. My GPA is 3.62 out of 5, and I've received the 
university scholarship four times throughout my undergraduate years.

In terms of technical skills, I'm proficient in C++ and Python, 
with hands-on experience in machine learning, algorithm design, 
and system-level performance optimization. I passed CET-6 and 
have the ability to read English academic literature independently.

My research experience is what I'm most proud of. In my junior year, 
under the supervision of Professor Lin Zhiyang, I studied the 
numerical solution of scalar hyperbolic conservation laws using 
deep learning. I compared Physics-Informed Neural Networks with 
Implicit Function Neural Networks on the Burgers equation and the 
LWR traffic flow model. This experience gave me deep exposure to 
partial differential equations — the same mathematical framework 
underlying diffusion equations and phase-field models in materials science.

For my graduation project, I independently designed and implemented 
a lightweight HNSW vector search engine in C++17, specifically 
optimized for the ARM architecture of the Apple M3 chip. 
The system achieved 7,104 query QPS with a recall rate of 92.4% 
on the SIFT1M benchmark. This project strengthened my understanding 
of hardware-software co-optimization and high-performance computing.

I'm applying to the School of Physical Science and Technology at 
SHANGHAITECH because I'm deeply interested in computational 
materials science — specifically, using machine learning potentials 
and data-driven methods to accelerate materials discovery. 
I believe the combination of my computational background and the 
cutting-edge materials research here will allow me to grow into 
a researcher who bridges these two fields.

That's all about me. Thank you for your time and attention.
```

**朗读时长参考**：约 115–130 秒（正常语速）

## ❓ 高频追问问题 + 参考答案

这些是老师根据你的自我介绍最可能追问的问题，每一条都和你说过的内容直接相关。

### Q1：Why do you choose to cross to materials science from computer science?

**参考答案：**

```text
That's a great question. My motivation comes from a realization 
I had during my PINN research. 

I was solving hyperbolic PDEs numerically, and I kept encountering 
the same mathematical structures — advection, diffusion, 
conservation — that appear in materials modeling. 
The Fick diffusion equation, the Cahn-Hilliard phase-field model, 
even heat transfer during thermal processing — they're all governed 
by the same type of equations I was working with.

That's when I realized: computational methods from computer science 
and the physical problems in materials science are two sides of 
the same coin. I want to be someone who can sit on both sides.

More practically, I think materials research is currently 
under-served by people who can write efficient code and build 
scalable systems. I want to bring that capability into the lab.
```

### Q2：What do you know about materials science? You have no formal background.

**参考答案（诚实 + 有准备感）：**

```text
You're right that I don't have a formal materials science degree. 
But I've been actively preparing for this transition.

I've studied the fundamentals — crystal structures, phase diagrams, 
common characterization techniques like XRD and SEM. 
I understand why the Fe-C phase diagram matters for steel processing, 
and I've read about the principles behind lithium-ion batteries.

I know there are large gaps in my materials knowledge, and I'm 
not pretending otherwise. But I believe my strength is in 
learning quickly and building rigorous quantitative understanding. 
If you give me a direction, I'll find the missing pieces.

What I can contribute right now is solid computational ability — 
numerical methods, machine learning, and high-performance 
implementation. I'm hoping to build the materials foundation 
alongside my graduate coursework.
```

### Q3：Tell me about your PINN research. What did you find?

**参考答案：**

```text
In that project, I compared two approaches for solving the 
Burgers equation — a classic nonlinear PDE with shock solutions.

The standard PINN method works well for smooth solutions, 
but struggles near shock discontinuities. The loss landscape 
becomes very ill-conditioned, and the network tends to smooth 
over the shock rather than capture it sharply.

The Implicit Function Neural Network, or IFNN, takes a different 
approach. Instead of directly approximating the solution, 
it learns an implicit function whose zero level set 
represents the solution surface. This gives it better ability 
to handle discontinuities, because it doesn't have to 
represent a sharp jump explicitly in the output.

My main takeaway was that for materials problems involving 
phase boundaries — which are essentially moving discontinuities — 
IFNN-type methods might outperform standard PINNs. 
That's actually a direction I'd like to explore further.
```

### Q4：What is your graduation project about? Why is it relevant here?

**参考答案：**

```text
My graduation project is a vector search engine based on 
the HNSW algorithm — Hierarchical Navigable Small World graphs — 
implemented in C++17 and optimized for the Apple M3's ARM architecture.

The core challenge was making approximate nearest-neighbor search 
extremely fast. I used SIMD intrinsics, fine-grained spinlocks, 
and a prefetching strategy to reduce memory latency. 
The result was 7,104 queries per second with 92.4% recall.

You might ask: what does this have to do with materials? 
The connection is through materials informatics. 

Databases like the Materials Project contain hundreds of thousands 
of computed material structures. A core problem is: 
given a target set of properties, find the most similar 
known material quickly. That's exactly a nearest-neighbor 
search problem in high-dimensional feature space — 
which is precisely what my engine is designed for.
```

### Q5：What research direction interests you at SHANGHAITECH?

**参考答案（需要你提前查导师，这里给通用框架）：**

```text
I'm most interested in computational materials science, 
specifically machine learning interatomic potentials — 
often called ML potentials or MLFFs.

Traditional molecular dynamics uses empirical force fields 
that sacrifice accuracy for speed, or ab initio methods 
that are accurate but too slow for large systems. 
ML potentials trained on DFT data offer a middle ground: 
near-DFT accuracy at a fraction of the cost.

I find this exciting because it's fundamentally a machine 
learning problem — designing architectures that respect 
physical symmetries, training on active learning strategies, 
and deploying efficiently. These are areas where my background 
is directly applicable.

I looked into Professor [X]'s work on [specific topic] — 
I found it particularly compelling because [specific reason]. 
I would love the chance to contribute to that direction.
```

> ⚠️ **注意**：最后这段一定要填入真实导师名字。复试前去上科大物质科学与技术学院官网，找 2–3 个方向对口的导师，把他们最近的论文标题和关键词记住。

### Q6：What are your strengths and weaknesses?

**优势参考答案：**

```text
My biggest strength is probably the combination of mathematical 
rigor and practical engineering ability.

Many people are good at theory or good at coding — 
I've trained myself to be comfortable in both. 
During my PINN research I was reading PDE analysis papers 
in the morning and debugging PyTorch code in the afternoon. 
During my graduation project I was reading CPU microarchitecture 
manuals and then writing assembly-level optimizations.

I think that kind of cross-domain fluency is exactly what's 
needed in computational materials research.
```

**缺点参考答案（诚实但可控）：**

```text
My biggest weakness is that I sometimes go too deep into 
implementation details before stepping back to see the bigger picture.

For example, in my graduation project, I spent two weeks 
optimizing a memory prefetching strategy that probably 
contributed maybe 5% of the final performance gain. 
In hindsight, those two weeks might have been better spent 
on higher-level algorithmic improvements.

I'm aware of this tendency now, and I've been practicing 
by setting explicit time-boxes for low-level optimization work 
before forcing myself to re-evaluate priorities.
```

### Q7：What is your plan during graduate school?

**参考答案：**

```text
My short-term plan for the first year is to close the gap 
in my materials science fundamentals — systematically study 
solid-state physics, thermodynamics, and materials characterization. 
I plan to do this in parallel with graduate coursework, 
so I can contribute meaningfully to the lab as quickly as possible.

In the medium term, I'd like to focus on a specific research problem 
at the intersection of machine learning and materials modeling — 
ideally something involving data-driven potential energy surfaces 
or property prediction.

Long-term, I hope to develop into a researcher who can bridge 
computational methods and experimental materials science — 
someone who can translate between the language of algorithms 
and the language of materials.
```

## ⚠️ 面试现场应急句

```text
没听清楚：
"I'm sorry, could you please repeat the question?"

没理解意思：
"I want to make sure I understand correctly — are you asking about [X]?"

答不上来时（不要沉默超过3秒）：
"That's an interesting question. I haven't studied that specific topic in depth yet, 
but from first principles, I would think..."

需要思考时：
"Let me think about that for just a moment."

结束回答：
"That's my current understanding — I'm happy to discuss further if you'd like."
```
